FREDDY FIX IT — ONBOARDING VOICEOVER  (v3)

One continuous take per video, read straight through as three sentences.
The timestamps are only there to show which frame is on screen while
that clause is spoken — the voice does not pause between them.

Evie reads at about 1.62 words/sec. Each script is ~50 words, which is
a little over 30 seconds raw and is pulled to exactly 30.00s with a
uniform ~1.09 tempo. Nothing is truncated. The closing card carries no
narration — it sits on music, so the last thing anyone hears is the
promise, not a URL.

READ STRAIGHT THROUGH:
"Posting a job takes about a minute — describe what's broken, add a
photo, and confirm your address. Local pros come back with real
estimates, so the choice is yours. Forty percent books it, and the
rest is paid only when the work is done, photographed before and
after, and you've said it's right."

Sentence one is how little we ask of them. Sentence two is that they're
the one choosing. Sentence three is the payoff — the money doesn't move
until there's photographic proof and they've said so themselves.

READ STRAIGHT THROUGH:
"Signing up takes a few minutes — pick the services you really do, add
your area and credentials, and turn on payouts before you bid. Quote a
real number, send the agreement first, photograph the work before and
after. No lead fees — you keep ninety-three percent, about a week after
it's finished."

Sentence one is the setup, in the order the form actually asks for it,
ending on the one step pros skip — payouts have to be on before a bid
can go anywhere. Sentence two is how to bid so the job holds together.
Sentence three is the payoff, and it is deliberately last: the money is
the reason to sit through the other two, and the week's wait is stated
plainly rather than discovered afterwards.

-------------------------------------------------------------
NOTES ON THE WORDING
-------------------------------------------------------------
"Forty percent" and "ninety-three percent" are spelled out rather than
written as numerals. Numerals and the web address are exactly where the
voice fumbled last time.

"the services you really do" is doing real work — a pro who ticks
everything gets leads they can't service, and stops opening our email.

Edit freely. Keep it as prose that reads well out loud, and keep each
one near 50 words; much over and the voice has to be sped up to fit 30s.
